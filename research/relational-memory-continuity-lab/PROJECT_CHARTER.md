# Relational Memory Continuity Lab — Project Charter

## 1. Research objective

Investigate whether **personalized, provenance-bound relational cueing** can improve access to autobiographical and relational continuity in people with mild cognitive impairment or Alzheimer's disease without fabricating memory, over-testing the person, or substituting machine confidence for clinical or family judgment.

## 2. Primary hypothesis

A personalized relational memory graph that adapts cue order to an individual's demonstrated response profile may support autobiographical/relational access better than a flat reminder list or non-personalized cue set.

This is a research hypothesis, not an efficacy claim.

## 3. Secondary hypotheses

1. **Cue-path heterogeneity:** Individuals differ materially in which cue modalities remain useful over time (for example voice, photograph, music, object, place, routine, semantic relationship, or temporal context).
2. **Adaptive cueing:** A system that learns which verified cue pathways are effective may outperform a fixed cue order.
3. **Relationship-graph value:** Preserving relationships among memory elements may provide more useful support than storing isolated autobiographical facts.
4. **Aphantasia transfer hypothesis:** Research on alternative memory strategies in aphantasia may generate useful, testable cueing strategies for dementia research, while aphantasia itself remains a distinct and non-neurodegenerative condition.
5. **Continuity over recall:** Outcomes centered on connection, orientation, conversation, and dignity may be more clinically/humanly meaningful than maximizing forced free recall alone.

## 4. Null and competing hypotheses

The project must actively preserve these alternatives:

- Personalized relational cueing provides no benefit over ordinary reminiscence or reminder systems.
- Any observed benefit is due only to increased attention, novelty, caregiver presence, or time-on-task.
- Personalization helps mood or engagement but not autobiographical access.
- Adaptive cueing overfits noisy responses and performs worse than simple fixed approaches.
- Repeated cueing increases confabulation or dependence on the external system.
- Benefits are limited to MCI and do not generalize to Alzheimer's disease.
- Different disease stages require fundamentally different approaches.
- Aphantasia research contributes little or nothing beyond existing dementia cueing literature.

A negative or null result is a successful research outcome.

## 5. Falsification conditions

The core hypothesis should be weakened or rejected if well-designed studies show that, relative to matched controls:

- relational-graph cueing does not improve prespecified meaningful outcomes;
- benefits disappear when attention/time/contact are controlled;
- false-memory/confabulation rates increase materially;
- distress or caregiver burden increases;
- adaptive cueing shows no reproducible advantage over simpler methods;
- cue-response maps are not stable enough to be useful;
- benefits cannot be reproduced independently.

## 6. Primary research questions

1. Which cue modalities most reliably support autobiographical or relational access at different stages of impairment?
2. Does cue order matter?
3. Does a relational graph add value beyond a collection of personalized media?
4. Can an individual's effective cue profile be estimated without turning interaction into a stressful memory test?
5. Can the system detect when a cue is harmful, confusing, stale, disputed, or ineffective and safely stop?
6. How should a system represent uncertainty and family disagreement about biographical facts?
7. How can cueing support emotional connection without falsely implying factual recognition?
8. What outcomes matter most to people living with cognitive decline and their families?

## 7. Required disciplinary review

Before any human-subject pilot, the project should seek review from relevant expertise such as:

- neurology / cognitive neurology;
- neuropsychology;
- geriatric psychiatry or geriatric medicine;
- occupational therapy / cognitive rehabilitation;
- dementia nursing and caregiving;
- speech-language pathology where relevant;
- human-computer interaction;
- clinical research methodology / biostatistics;
- research ethics;
- privacy and health-data governance;
- people living with MCI/dementia and family/care partners.

## 9. Success criteria for the incubation phase

Before prototype promotion, RMCL should have:

- a systematic or scoping literature map;
- explicit evidence-for and evidence-against tables;
- a taxonomy of cue modalities and likely confounders;
- a provenance model for autobiographical claims;
- a false-memory and distress threat model;
- at least one preregisterable experimental design;
- clinically meaningful candidate outcomes;
- a data-minimization/privacy plan;
- an expert-review plan;
- a decision on whether the relational-graph concept adds enough novelty over existing reminiscence/external-memory research to justify further work.

## 10. Governance principle

The project borrows evidence-integrity and authority-separation ideas from Intercommunication Enhancements, but those ideas enter RMCL only as design lineage. They do not confer clinical validity, research ethics approval, medical authority, or permission to work with vulnerable participants.
