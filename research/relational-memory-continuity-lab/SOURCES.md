# Relational Memory Continuity Lab — Initial Evidence Base

**Status:** starting bibliography, not a systematic review.  
**Rule:** source presence does not equal validation of the RMCL hypothesis.

## Aphantasia / alternate retrieval-path literature

### Monzel et al. — hippocampal-occipital connectivity and autobiographical memory in aphantasia

- PubMed: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/39325034/
- Finding relevant to RMCL: participants with congenital aphantasia reported more autobiographical-memory difficulty and fewer internal/emotional details; neural differences involved hippocampal and visual-perceptual systems.
- RMCL use: supports studying alternate retrieval architectures when vivid visual imagery is weak.
- Does **not** establish: that aphantasia is analogous to Alzheimer's disease or that aphantasia-derived strategies transfer clinically.

### Dawes et al. — remembering the past and imagining the future with aphantasia

- PubMed: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/35752014/
- Finding relevant to RMCL: aphantasic participants generated fewer episodic details and showed reduced phenomenological richness, especially visual detail.
- RMCL use: supports the distinction between vivid visual re-experiencing and other possible routes to autobiographical knowledge.

### Zeman — imagery vividness extremes review

- PubMed: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/38548492/
- Finding relevant to RMCL: aphantasia is variably associated with reduced autobiographical memory and likely has subtypes; evidence is still developing.
- RMCL use: reinforces the need to avoid treating aphantasia as a single uniform mechanism.

### McCormick & Lange — autobiographical memory in aphantasia and congenital blindness

- PubMed: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42339253/
- Finding relevant to RMCL: review argues that scene construction may matter more than visual encoding alone and discusses auditory/tactile routes in congenital blindness.
- RMCL use: motivates multimodal cue-route hypotheses.
- Status: hypothesis-generating review, not direct dementia evidence.

## Alzheimer's / dementia cueing and rehabilitation literature

### Clare & Woods-era memory stimulation review in Alzheimer's disease

- PubMed: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/12724453/
- Relevant interventions reviewed: errorless learning, spaced retrieval, vanishing cues, encoding specificity, cognitive support at retrieval, and external memory aids.
- RMCL use: establishes that cue-based memory rehabilitation in Alzheimer's disease is not a new concept and must be treated as prior art.

### Woods et al. — Cochrane review of reminiscence therapy for dementia

- PubMed: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/29493789/
- Relevant point: reminiscence therapy uses prompts such as photographs, music, objects, and other personal material to stimulate memories and conversation.
- RMCL use: major comparison baseline.
- Claim boundary: effects across quality of life, communication, cognition, and mood are generally small/inconsistent and vary by modality and setting.

### Subramaniam & Woods — individual reminiscence systematic review

- PubMed: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/22550983/
- Relevant point: personalized life review, life-story books, and multimedia approaches showed some psychosocial promise, whereas general reminiscence materials did not show comparable evidence in the reviewed studies.
- RMCL use: supports testing personalization specifically.

### Yasuda et al. — personalized reminiscence photo videos

- PubMed: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/19235628/
- Relevant point: in a small study, most participants attended more to personalized reminiscence photo videos than comparison TV content.
- RMCL use: supports personalized multimedia as an established adjacent approach.
- Claim boundary: very small sample; attention is not proof of autobiographical retrieval or clinical efficacy.

### El Haj et al./systematic review of autobiographical photographs in older adults

- PubMed: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/35859072/
- Relevant point: autobiographical-photograph interventions were associated in included trials with some improvements in well-being, quality of life, identity, and cognitive outcomes.
- RMCL use: supports photographs as one cue modality, not necessarily the dominant one.

## Digital cognitive intervention literature

### Chan et al. — computerized cognitive training for memory in MCI/dementia

- PubMed: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/38172429/
- Relevant point: meta-analysis found memory improvements in MCI across verbal, visual, and working memory outcomes; dementia evidence was more limited/heterogeneous.
- RMCL use: supports digital intervention feasibility while preserving population-stage uncertainty.

### Digital cognitive interventions vs active controls/usual care — 2026 systematic review/meta-analysis

- PubMed: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42356174/
- Relevant point: digital interventions improved global cognition compared with pooled controls, with clearer effects in MCI; added benefit over active controls was uncertain.
- RMCL use: warns that comparison against usual care alone can exaggerate apparent novelty/value.

### Moura et al. — digital episodic memory tools for identifying amnestic MCI

- PubMed: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42190978/
- Relevant point: current digital episodic-memory tooling is an active research area, including detection/assessment.
- RMCL use: maintain strict separation between **support/rehabilitation** and **diagnostic identification**.

## Initial evidence conclusion

The current literature supports only the following cautious statement:

> Personalized, multimodal cueing and external memory support already have meaningful precedent in dementia care and cognitive rehabilitation, while aphantasia and related natural experiments provide plausible hypotheses about alternate retrieval pathways. The specific RMCL idea—provenance-aware relational memory graphs with adaptive cue-path selection—remains unvalidated and must demonstrate value beyond personalization, reminiscence therapy, and ordinary digital memory aids.
